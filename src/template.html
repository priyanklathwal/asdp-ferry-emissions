What the Sunda Strait ferries burn

Every twelve minutes or so, a ferry leaves Merak for Bakauheni. It is fifteen nautical miles. Here is what that costs in energy, in carbon, and in the air over two Indonesian port towns — and what would actually change it.

Read this before using any number on this page. Emission factors, auxiliary power demand, low-load penalties, hull fouling and the phase structure are transcribed from the published inventory literature and carry a SOURCED tag. What remains analyst judgement carries ASSUM, and the largest of those is installed engine power: the register supplied has no kW field, so power is estimated from hull dimensions. Nine sanity checks against independent benchmarks are reported below and all currently pass.

WHERE THIS LANDS

One crossing, roughly the annual emissions of a small coal unit

WHAT THE DATA ACTUALLY CONTAINS

The inventory and the corridor barely overlap

The covering note describes the worktab as a sub-set of vessels operated or owned by ASDP. It is broader than that. Of the vessels in the register, only a minority carry an ASDP corporate owner or operator, and only a handful of them appear on the Merak–Bakauheni crossing at all in the six-and-a-half months of movement data.

The corridor is served by a larger and partly different set of ships. That matters for framing: a fleet-replacement question asked of the register is a different question from a decarbonisation question asked of the corridor.

BASELINE

Slow, short, and constantly repeated

The crossing is about 15 nautical miles. Observed port-to-port times cluster around three hours, which implies an average speed well below the design speed of these hulls. Part of that is genuine slow steaming; part is queueing offshore for a berth, which the movement data cannot separate from sailing. The model treats it as low-load steaming with a floor on propulsion power, and the size of that floor is the largest single uncertainty here.

On Indonesia's grid, electrifying these ferries would raise emissions and cut costs

A note on terminology: SAF is an aviation fuel and has no shipping equivalent by that name. The marine analogues are FAME biodiesel, which Indonesia already blends to B40 on land, HVO renewable diesel, and bio- or e-methanol.

THE DECIDING NUMBER

This is the argument for treating ferry electrification as a power-sector project rather than a shipping project. The vessels are a captive, highly predictable load at two fixed points, which is close to ideal for a dedicated solar-plus-storage build at the terminals. ASDP is already installing large solar arrays at both ports for terminal load. The question worth putting to them is whether that programme can be sized against a charging load instead.

HOW MUCH TO TRUST THIS

The structural uncertainty is now small; the data gap is not

Resolving the phase structure against the vessels' own fastest crossings, rather than imposing an arbitrary floor on engine load, has cut the structural spread from about ±15% to under 7%. What remains is a data gap, not a modelling choice: installed engine power is absent from the register, and positional AIS would replace the phase inference entirely.
